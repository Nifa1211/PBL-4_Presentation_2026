Comparative Analysis of Deep Learning Models for Face Anti-Spoofing in Secure Biometric Authentication

A rigorous, unified comparison of three CNN architectures — MobileNetV2, ResNet50, and EfficientNetB0 — evaluated across accuracy, AUC, efficiency, and deployment viability, culminating in a clear, evidence-backed recommendation for real-world integration into live biometric authentication systems.

Problem Statement

Facial biometric authentication is deployed at scale across banking, mobile access, and physical security. Yet the same camera-based convenience that makes it popular also makes it exploitable — attackers bypass these systems using printed photographs, looped video replays, or AI-generated deepfakes that are growing cheaper and more convincing by the day.

Conventional detection methods are no longer sufficient. What the field needs is a deep learning-based anti-spoofing layer that is not just accurate in controlled settings, but reliable, fast, and efficient enough to operate in real time on real hardware. The problem is that no study provides a fair, head-to-head comparison of candidate architectures against this deployment standard.

Core Research Aim

Benchmark MobileNetV2, ResNet50 & EfficientNetB0 under an identical experimental framework — and identify the single architecture best suited for production deployment in a real-world face anti-spoofing system.

System Methodology

Dataset & Preprocessing

Kaggle's Deepfake & Real Images dataset — 112K training / 28K validation samples, balanced across real and fake classes. Images resized to 224×224 px, normalised to [0,1]. Augmentation: random horizontal flips, brightness (±15%), contrast (0.85–1.15). A tf.data pipeline with batching, prefetching, and caching ensures consistent GPU throughput across all model runs.

Three Models, One Framework

MobileNetV2 ~3.4M params

Depthwise separable convolutions — designed for edge devices and real-time inference

ResNet50 ~25M params

Residual connections — deep feature extraction, strong but computationally heavy

EfficientNetB0 ~5.3M params

Compound scaling — theoretically balanced, sensitive to fine-tuning conditions

Results & Analysis

MobileNetV2 was the clear standout — 82.38% accuracy and 90.98% AUC, with metrics improving steadily across all three epochs. Its lightweight design enabled training in just 8.4 minutes with strong generalisation to unseen data, confirming its suitability for real-world deployment.

ResNet50 showed solid feature extraction, reaching 77.74% accuracy and 87.31% AUC, with notably strong recall (89.02%). However, at 20.2 minutes training time — 2.4× longer than MobileNetV2 — and 25M parameters, it exceeds the compute budget for real-time or edge applications.

EfficientNetB0 failed to converge. Accuracy flatlined at ~50%, precision and recall stayed at zero, and AUC of 0.55 reflected near-random classification — indicating underfitting due to insufficient fine-tuning depth under the constrained training budget.

Deployment Verdict

Selected for Deployment

MobileNetV2

Highest accuracy (82.38%) and best AUC (90.98%) with the fastest training time (8.4 min). Its lightweight structure, stable convergence, and strong generalisation make it the ideal backbone for real-time mobile and edge-device deployment without sacrificing detection reliability.

Real-time · Mobile · Edge-ready
Viable with Trade-offs

ResNet50

Strong recall (89.02%) and solid AUC make it a capable detector, but 25M parameters and 20+ minutes of training time place it outside practical reach for real-time systems. Best suited to server-side or offline use cases where compute is not a constraint.

Server-side · Offline · High compute
Not Viable (Current Config)

EfficientNetB0

Despite theoretical advantages, EfficientNetB0 failed to learn meaningful patterns under constrained 3-epoch training — stalling at 50% accuracy. Its compound scaling demands deeper fine-tuning and a more diverse dataset to unlock its potential.

Needs: Extended training · Deeper fine-tuning
